In plain English

What is a "sheet switch"?
A scroll is a spiral of papyrus sheet. When we trace a surface through the CT scan, the trace can accidentally hop from one wind of the spiral onto the wind next to it, like a needle skipping a groove on a record. A detector's job is to notice where a traced surface (a "patch") does that, so the mistake can be fixed before anyone reads text off the wrong sheet.
What does "recall" mean here?
Of all the confirmed sheet switches we know about in the test set, recall is the fraction a detector actually flagged (hit) within 1.0 mm of the switch. Higher is better. The 95% interval (Wilson score) shows how much that number could plausibly move with more test data.
What is "false alarms / 100 mm"?
How often a detector cries wolf on parts of the surface we've confirmed have no switch, per 100 mm of that confirmed-clean surface. Lower is better.
What is "coverage"?
How many of the test patches a detector even produced a verdict for. Recall still counts an event on a patch a detector never looked at as a miss — that's unavoidable if recall is to mean "fraction of all known switches caught." Coverage is what tells you whether a low recall means the detector looked and missed, or mostly didn't look: a detector that only covers a handful of patches will show a low recall for that reason alone, not necessarily because it performs badly where it does run.
How do I submit a detector?
Run your detector, score its output with the scorer kit, then open a Detector submission issue with your alarms file and the exact command you ran — see CONTRIBUTING.md. We re-score everyone's alarms ourselves before publishing a row.

Coverage matters: a detector with few patches with a verdict is limited in scope here, not wrong — it gets a Limited scope badge (fewer than half the patches got a verdict) and sorts after full-coverage detectors at the same recall, so a low false-alarm rate earned on a sliver of the corpus doesn't out-rank one earned on all of it. The false-alarm rate also gets its own 95% confidence interval, the same patch-block bootstrap used for recall, so its rate is judged with the same rigor. Every row is reproducible with the scorer kit on the same inputs.

Score your own detector

Pick an alarms.json file from your computer. Nothing is uploaded — scoring runs entirely in your browser with the same corpus data this page already loaded.

Format: {"patch_id": [[x, y, z], ...], ...} (level-2 voxel coordinates, one list of alarm points per patch; a patch you looked at but raised no alarm on should still be a key, mapped to an empty list).

False alarms per 100 mm need the confirmed-clean patch geometry, which this page does not ship. Run the scorer kit for the full report, including false alarms:

python -m tools.switchbench_kit score --corpus results/switchbench_natural_v0_events.json \
  --alarms alarms.json --patches-dir DIR

Data: leaderboard.json + corpus_events.json, written by tools/release/export_viewer_data.py. Source: this repository's tools/switchbench_kit.
